# The semantic spectrum, and what "reasoning motifs" should mean here

Status: working note, 2026-10-05. Records a clarification from Brian; the mapping in section 3 is a hypothesis to test, not a finding.

## 1. What Brian means by the semantic spectrum

Thinking something through runs along one range of formality: from a rough list of the concepts that matter, through taxonomies and typed relations, to a fully operational, executable model, or beyond (simulated, then verified). A given idea can sit anywhere on it, and working an idea moves it along the range.

This is the published **ontology spectrum** (also called the semantic spectrum). The ecosystem's own treatment is Axis 4 ("formalization") of `vision/wiki/synthesis/formalization-megamodel-and-selection.md` in the `vision` repository (page status: archived, authority: derived, as of 2026-10-05). Levels there, light to heavy: catalog, glossary, thesaurus, informal is-a, formal is-a, formal instances, frames with value restrictions, general logical constraints; past those, behavioural/simulated and verified. "Beyond" is not one direction: models can add dynamic, intentional (goals) and social (actors) aspects.

## 2. Where inquiry-graph sits

Per-chat graphs reach typed relations over claims, questions and methods with verbatim anchors: roughly "frames", with no reasoner deriving new facts. The topic digests are closer to a catalog of concepts with evidence. Neither is executable.

## 3. Hypothesis: a reasoning method is a move along the spectrum, or a composition of such moves

Brian's "canonical factorization" is the move of reducing a whole to a small set of primitives that other things compose from. Examples he gave (policy-analysis methods): qualitative coding, statistics, simulation, elicitation; methods such as process tracing or grounded theory as compositions of them. Under this reading:
- Moving up the spectrum: define a thing by role, factor into primitives, formalize a relation.
- Moving down: ask for a concrete example, ground an abstraction in a case.
- Testing by action: treat a frame as a hypothesis and test it by intervention (the executable end).
- Policies ("use what exists", "reuse my own repo") are prescriptive rules, kept separate from methods.

Three layers, none of them fixed bins: instances (what he thought about a specific thing), methods (general moves), policies (prescriptions). Where a method is cut from its instances is itself a choice that stays revisable.

## 4. Consequence for mining motifs

Mine upward from instances (short own-words messages, paste-filtered), cluster to patterns, then to methods, keep each cluster tied to its evidence, and tag each method with the direction it moves (up, down, test) where that is clear. A method counts only if its instances recur across topics and years. Brian decides which stay.
