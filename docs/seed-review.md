## Analyst concerns—not retrospectively attributed to the participants

**Sufficiency of a stable world.** The early pseudo-proof treats stability as if it directly licenses projection and inversion. Stability alone does not specify sampling, measurement bias, model identifiability, or the target distribution. Keep `three-way-sketch` a hypothesis and the induction/abduction foundations open. Its `supports` edge records the proposed argument, not a validated warrant.

**Information versus representation.** The dots discussion rightly highlights a cognitively useful transformation from listed points to a relational description. But if separation is entailed by a complete finite point description, making it explicit need not be logically ampliative. Also, deterministic processing of the same input does not create new Shannon information about an external target under the usual data-processing assumptions. Do not equate cognitive usefulness, compression, logical novelty, and information gain.

**Exhaustiveness.** The discussion did not prove deduction/induction/abduction exhaustive. A definition of three tags in a schema cannot supply the missing theorem. The seed leaves inference-family tags unspecified rather than classifying every move into the disputed taxonomy.

**Induction first.** The initial loop hypothesis and later recurrent formulation are both preserved. The graph does not infer that induction must always occur first in learning or cognition.

**Existing ontologies.** Earlier replies overstated confidence that a missing universal inquiry ontology had been established. The implemented vocabulary is a pragmatic V1 selection aligned with known frameworks, not a novelty claim or proof that no comprehensive prior ontology exists.

**Hidden state identification.** A public conversation supports an annotation of expressed moves, not a uniquely identified internal mental model. This applies to humans and language models. A diagram is not a measurement of actual private cognition.

**Scope corrections.** The user's request for a reasoning-history map refined, rather than rejected, the preference for typed graphs. Similarly, challenging a designer-first account of learning narrows its applicability; it does not imply communication design is meaningless. The curation uses those distinctions rather than treating every pivot as wholesale falsification.
