# Primary references and alignment boundary

Checked during V1 development, 2026-09-27. These references informed the design; they do not certify that the custom JSON is an implementation of each standard.

- **Inference Anchoring Theory quick-start guide**, ARG-tech: https://arg.tech/~chris/acl2019tut/IAT-guidelines.pdf . Distinguishes informational/argumentative structure, locutions, transitions and anchoring; supports linked premises and attacks on inference relations. Particularly relevant: an assertion in the dialogue is not proof of sincere belief. The PDF and its diagrams were inspected.
- **IAT-related argument parsing**, primary proceedings paper: https://aclanthology.org/2024.argmining-1.13/ . Relevant comparator for extracting argument/dialogue structure; not a claim that our extra inquiry-state semantics are already standardized.
- **Coherence of Argumentative Dialogue Snippets: A New Method for Large Scale Evaluation with an Application to Inference Anchoring Theory**, Piwek, Amidei and Stoyanchev (2025): https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.758/ . Relevant evaluation literature. No numerical performance claims are imported into this project.
- **PROV-O**, W3C Recommendation: https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/ . Entity, Activity, Agent and qualified provenance inform the separation of content, moves and participants.
- **Web Annotation Data Model**, W3C Recommendation: https://www.w3.org/TR/annotation-model/ . Text quote/position selectors inform our grounded source spans; V1 uses a smaller custom representation and Unicode-offset convention.
- **SKOS Reference**, W3C: https://www.w3.org/TR/skos-reference/ . Relevant for controlled vocabularies and concept relationships, not sufficient on its own for inquiry-state change.
- **LangExtract**, official Google repository: https://github.com/google/langextract . Source-grounded structured extraction is closely related. Mention extraction and source grounding do not themselves ensure a coherent, attributed, temporally consistent inquiry graph.
- **Pydantic JSON Schema**, official documentation: https://docs.pydantic.dev/latest/concepts/json_schema/ . Executable models generate committed JSON Schemas.
- **OpenAI structured outputs**, official documentation: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs . The optional adapter uses the SDK's Pydantic structured-output parsing interface. Schema adherence does not establish semantic fidelity, and refusal/incomplete cases still require handling.
- **Johan de Kleer, “An assumption-based TMS,” Artificial Intelligence 28(2), 127–162 (1986)**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(86)90080-9 . Primary precedent for maintaining multiple assumption environments, explicit justifications and minimal support labels without selecting one globally committed theory. The project borrows the architectural distinction; it does not claim ATMS conformance.
- **Joseph A. Goguen and Rod M. Burstall, “Institutions: Abstract Model Theory for Specification and Programming,” Journal of the ACM 39(1), 95–146 (1992)**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/147508.147524 . Primary precedent for separating signatures, sentences, models and satisfaction and for using signature morphisms to control language/model translation.
- **Robert Harper, Furio Honsell and Gordon Plotkin, “A Framework for Defining Logics,” Journal of the ACM 40(1), 143–184 (1993)**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/138027.138060 . LF represents syntax, judgments, rules and proofs in a typed meta-language; relevant as a foundation that can sit inside a modular representation system rather than as a separate epistemic layer.
- **Florian Rabe, “The MMT Language”**, https://www.kwarc.info/people/frabe/Research/rabe_mmt_09.pdf . MMT supplies a foundation-independent module layer based on theories, symbols/objects and theory morphisms. It motivates collapsing several earlier candidate-artifact categories into a smaller formal representation substrate.
- **Falk Lieder and Thomas L. Griffiths, “Strategy selection as rational metareasoning,” Psychological Review 124(6), 762–794 (2017)**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000075 . Formal precedent for treating strategy choice as a metareasoning problem. The current project borrows the strategy-selection distinction but does not assume expected-value optimization in its base definition.
- **Wiebe van der Hoek, John-Jules Meyer and Jan Treur, “Formal semantics of meta-level architectures: Temporal epistemic reflection,” International Journal of Intelligent Systems 18(12), 1293–1317 (2003)**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/int.10139 . Relevant precedent for formal object/meta architectures and upward/downward reflection.
- **Harald Rueß, Holger Pfeifer and F. W. von Henke, “Formalization and Reasoning in a Reflective Architecture” (IJCAI'95 Workshop)**, https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.inst.090/Publikationen/1995/Ruess95FormalizationAndReasoning.pdf . Relevant example of making object-level formal artifacts first-class at a meta-level and applying meta-operators through reflection principles.
- **A Validated Ontology for Metareasoning in Intelligent Systems** (2022), https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/10/4/113 . Relevant ontology precedent for distinguishing object-level reasoning from meta-level monitoring/control tasks.
- **Sumit Gulwani, Oleksandr Polozov and Rishabh Singh, “Program Synthesis,” Foundations and Trends in Programming Languages 4(1–2), 1–119 (2017)**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1561/2500000010 . Broad synthesis reference for separating specification/representation of candidate programs, search spaces and synthesis procedures; motivates the draft-space/operator/strategy distinction without implying all reasoning is program synthesis.
- **Armando Solar-Lezama et al., “Combinatorial Sketching for Finite Programs,” ASPLOS (2006)**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/1168857.1168907 . Early counterexample-guided/sketch-based synthesis precedent for iterating candidate construction with verifier feedback.
- **Antonis Kakas, Robert Kowalski and Francesca Toni, “Abductive Logic Programming,” Journal of Logic and Computation 2(6), 719–770 (1992)**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/logcom/2.6.719 . Formal precedent for constraining generated explanatory hypotheses by abducibles and integrity conditions rather than treating abduction as one unconstrained primitive leap.
- **Sergei Artemov, “The Logic of Justification,” The Review of Symbolic Logic 1(4), 477–513 (2008)**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S1755020308090060 . Primary precedent for representing explicit justification terms `t:F` rather than hiding reasons inside an implicit epistemic modality. Relevant to the support/certificate side of the warrant layer.
- **Phan Minh Dung, “On the Acceptability of Arguments and its Fundamental Role in Nonmonotonic Reasoning, Logic Programming and n-Person Games,” Artificial Intelligence 77(2), 321–357 (1995)**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(94)00041-X . Primary abstract-argumentation precedent for acceptability under attack/defense; relevant to defeasible warrant and loss of license under defeating information.
- **Inigo Incer, “The Algebra of Contracts,” UC Berkeley EECS Technical Report UCB/EECS-2022-99 (2022)**, https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2022/EECS-2022-99.html . Modern formal reference for assume-guarantee contracts `(A,G)`, where assumptions constrain the environment and guarantees state conditional commitments. This closely matches the assumptions/guarantee factor in the project’s warrant judgment.
- **Formal Learning Theory**, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/learning-formal/ . Survey reference for method-level convergence and statistical-learning guarantees under explicit problem-class assumptions; relevant to warrants for inductive methods rather than only proposition-level support.
- **Dynamic Epistemic Logic**, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dynamic-epistemic/ . Relevant precedent for explicit epistemic action models with preconditions and model-transforming product-update semantics. Supports treating action as a structured transformer rather than a fixed folk verb taxonomy.
- **Hirofumi Katsuno and Alberto O. Mendelzon, “On the Difference Between Updating a Knowledge Base and Revising It,”** in *Belief Revision* (1992), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511526664.007 . Important distinction between revision from new information about a static world and update reflecting change in the world.
- **Todd J. Green, Grigoris Karvounarakis and Val Tannen, “Provenance Semirings,” PODS 2007, 31–40**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/1265530.1265535 . Primary precedent for symbolic provenance with addition as alternative derivation and multiplication as joint use, followed by homomorphic interpretation into application-specific annotation semirings.
- **Kathryn Blackmond Laskey and Paul E. Lehner, “Assumptions, Beliefs and Probabilities,” Artificial Intelligence 41(1), 65–77 (1989)**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(89)90078-7 . Shows how probabilities on ATMS assumptions can yield beliefs while preserving dependence through label structure; relevant to separating symbolic support provenance from numeric support.
- **Weiru Liu and Alan Bundy, “Constructing Probabilistic ATMSs Using Extended Incidence Calculus,” International Journal of Approximate Reasoning 15(2), 145–182 (1996)**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/0888-613X(96)00031-X . Further formal bridge between ATMS labels/justifications and probabilistic reasoning over assumptions.
- **Bayesian Epistemology**, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology-bayesian/ . Relevant here primarily for Jeffrey conditionalization/probability kinematics as an example of a graded update rule whose applicability depends on explicit invariance and evidential assumptions.

IBIS, discourse-relation frameworks, proof provenance, richer belief revision/update algebras, negative/defeasible provenance, probabilistic truth-maintenance extensions and richer strategy-episode ontologies remain potential alignment investigations. This release does not claim to have exhaustively searched all existing inquiry ontologies, does not assert that no integrated ontology exists, and does not fabricate an interoperability mapping where semantics have not been checked.
