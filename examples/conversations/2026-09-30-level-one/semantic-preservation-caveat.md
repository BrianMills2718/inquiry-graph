# Semantic preservation caveat for adoption/deletion work

## Why this matters

Replacing a local construct with a mature donor is not justified by similar names or a plausible field-to-field correspondence.

Formal preservation results are always relative to a formalization. A theorem can establish that a translation preserves a formal predicate, judgment, query answer or behavior. It does not by itself establish that the formalization exhausts the ordinary-language or conceptual meaning of the thing being represented.

Therefore:

> **artifact/schema translation does not imply identity of meaning, just as it does not imply guarantee transport.**

## Replacement claims are themselves epistemic claims

A deletion/adoption statement should be read as:

> For purpose P and relevant distinctions D, donor representation B is adequate to replace local representation A to strength G, under assumptions Phi and evidence/certificate kappa.

That replacement claim needs warrant.

Possible warrant strengths include:

1. formal satisfaction/judgment/query preservation;
2. refinement, simulation/bisimulation or verified behavioral equivalence;
3. sound abstraction with explicit information loss;
4. validated competency-question/query equivalence;
5. empirical conformance tests/counterexamples;
6. expert-curated ontology alignment;
7. heuristic/provisional correspondence.

These are not interchangeable.

## Task-relative adequacy

The practical criterion is not metaphysical identity of meaning.

For a declared consumer/query set Q, ask whether the donor preserves the distinctions and behaviors on which that consumer relies.

If two local states differ in a way relevant to Q, the donor mapping must not silently collapse them.

If the donor cannot formally preserve the distinction but the project accepts a weaker empirical/expert correspondence, that weaker status must remain visible.

## Natural-language/conceptual boundary

For concepts whose meaning is partly natural-language, social, contextual, pragmatic or contested, complete formal semantic equivalence may be unavailable in principle or in practice.

Do not invent a local theory of meaning to close that gap.

Use mature ontology-alignment/semantic-interoperability/conceptual-modeling machinery where applicable, state the purpose and evidence for the correspondence, preserve uncertainty, and re-warrant downstream claims when needed.

## Deletion rule

A local construct may be deleted only when:

1. the relevant observable/interface contract is stated;
2. the donor mapping preserves that contract to an explicitly stated strength;
3. known losses and assumptions are recorded;
4. existing fixtures/conformance tests still produce the distinctions, refusals and outcomes that matter;
5. any weaker natural-language/conceptual alignment is not mislabeled as formal equivalence.

If these conditions are not established, classify the mapping as a candidate alignment rather than a completed deletion.
