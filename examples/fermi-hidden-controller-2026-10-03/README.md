# Fermi hidden-controller inquiry — 2026-10-03

This directory records the source layer for a speculative Fermi-paradox inquiry developed in conversation.

## Central question

Conditional on a technologically overwhelming extraterrestrial civilization existing and being able to observe or influence Earth, what combination of game theory, thermodynamic efficiency, ASI transition dynamics, information value, and moral constraints could explain why humanity remains intact and why the civilization remains unobserved?

This is a hypothesis-building record, **not evidence that extraterrestrial controllers exist**.

## Inquiry trajectory

1. **Covert predictability.** Disclosure could convert humanity from a system being modeled into an adversarial strategic actor that models, deceives, and randomizes against the controller.
2. **ASI transition.** Secrecy may matter most before an intelligence explosion because disclosure gives a future peer-level strategist inherited information about the controller.
3. **Multiple advanced actors.** Monopoly, coordination, convergence, and defection were identified as competing game-theoretic regimes.
4. **Convergent intent vs historical lock-in.** Similar mature behavior could arise from moral/instrumental convergence or from a first mover imposing a durable strategic order.
5. **Entropy as instrumental constraint.** The working claim was narrowed from “minimize entropy” to avoiding unnecessary entropy increase and preserving usable free energy/optionality while pursuing whatever terminal values exist.
6. **Replacement problem.** A strong consequentialist optimizer may have reason to replace biological Earth with more efficient value-producing computation. This is the main moral obstacle.
7. **Natural physical computation.** Earth may be useful because reality is already running a physical process that a separate simulation would have to reproduce at additional physical cost.
8. **Candidate information value.** The strongest proposed output is an independent sample of the path life → intelligence → technology → ASI, especially convergence, strategy, values, and rare failure modes.
9. **Marginal-sample objection.** An ancient civilization should already have many samples; another Earth must have enough rare-tail/model-error value to justify its opportunity cost.
10. **Isolation architecture.** Natural and seeded civilizations could in principle be staggered to avoid overlapping technological-detection windows; biosignatures are a different information channel.
11. **Silence is information.** Suppression cannot make observers informationally blank. The Great Silence itself shifts beliefs about rarity, Great Filters, and mature civilization behavior.
12. **Dyson-sphere challenge.** If usable-resource preservation dominates, leaving starlight uncaptured is costly. This prevents entropy efficiency alone from explaining why Earth is preserved.
13. **Current frontier.** Infer what non-deontological or otherwise convergent value system could make preservation of Earth worth its resource opportunity cost while still favoring predictive, minimal, covert management.

## Current working architecture

The cleanest separation reached in the conversation is:

- **Ethics / terminal value:** must explain why Earth or its inhabitants are worth preserving rather than replacing.
- **Thermodynamic efficiency:** favors avoiding unnecessary irreversible expenditure and preserving usable resources/optionality.
- **Prediction:** allows goals to be achieved with less intervention and wasted action.
- **Secrecy / game theory:** can preserve predictive advantage by preventing the target from becoming strategically responsive to the controller.
- **ASI:** may be the phase transition at which informational asymmetry becomes especially valuable.
- **Natural computation / experimentation:** is a possible additional reason to let independent civilizations develop, but its marginal value remains unresolved.

## Open questions

- What value systems—especially non-deontological ones—make preserving existing biological civilizations preferable to optimized replacement?
- Can moral convergence be defended strongly enough to solve the multi-civilization defection problem?
- What information from an independently evolving civilization remains valuable to a mature ASI after many prior samples?
- When is letting a physical system evolve cheaper than simulating or engineering equivalent search diversity?
- How much intervention can occur before the “independent sample” rationale becomes self-defeating?
- What observation policy best preserves strategic underdetermination when silence itself conveys information?
- Does a resource-conserving mature civilization generically imply Dyson-scale stellar capture, and if so what observation could justify exceptions?
